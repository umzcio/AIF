# UM_Standards — Faculty & Researchers

Reference file for faculty, instructors, graduate students, and research staff at the
University of Montana. Load this file when the user's context involves teaching, research,
grants, IRB, student interactions, or academic publishing.

---

## FERPA — Student Data

FERPA protects the education records of all enrolled and formerly enrolled students.

### What You Cannot Do With Claude

- Share student names alongside grades, GPA, attendance, or academic standing
- Paste gradebook exports, LMS data, or course roster details with identifiable information
- Analyze disciplinary, financial aid, or accommodation records tied to named students
- Use Claude to make or inform decisions about individual student standing without removing
  identifying information first

### What You Can Do

- Use anonymized or fictional student examples for pedagogy development
- Draft feedback templates (then personalize manually)
- Develop rubrics, assignment descriptions, and course policies
- Analyze aggregate, de-identified course outcome data

**Rule of thumb**: If a student could identify themselves in the data you're sharing,
it's FERPA-protected. Remove names, student IDs, and any combination of details that
could re-identify an individual before using Claude.

## Research Data Standards

### IRB and Human Subjects Data

- Raw data from IRB-approved studies involving human subjects should not be processed
  through Claude without confirming your IRB protocol permits use of third-party AI tools
- De-identified data may be appropriate — check your protocol's definition of de-identification
- If your study involves Indigenous participants or tribal communities, see the CARE
  Principles section in the core SKILL.md — additional tribal nation authorization may
  be required regardless of IRB approval

### Unpublished Research

- Do not share unpublished datasets, pre-publication manuscripts, or grant-pending findings
  in ways that could create prior disclosure or IP concerns
- Claude conversations are not guaranteed confidential — treat them like email
- For proprietary or export-controlled research, consult the Research Office before
  using Claude in your workflow

## Grant Writing with Claude

Claude is well-suited to assist with grant development. Follow these guidelines:

### Disclosure Requirements by Funder

| Funder | AI Policy (as of early 2026) |
|---|---|
| NSF | Disclose AI use in proposal development per current NSF policy |
| NIH | Check current NOT policies; disclosure expectations evolving |
| DOE | Review solicitation language; disclosure often required |
| DOEd (FIPSE, Title III) | Follow solicitation guidance; disclose when substantive |
| Private foundations | Varies — check RFP or contact program officer |

**When in doubt, disclose.** A brief acknowledgment is always safer than omission.

### Safe Grant Writing Practices

- Use Claude to draft narrative sections, then revise substantially with your own expertise
- Do not share reviewer feedback from previous submissions (may violate funder confidentiality)
- Budget justifications involving named personnel salaries: anonymize before using Claude
- Subcontract details with partner institutions: treat as confidential until agreements are signed

## Academic AI Disclosure — Teaching Contexts

UM's default position is transparency. When using Claude to develop course materials:

- **Syllabus and policy documents**: Disclose AI assistance; students should know how
  course materials are developed
- **Lecture content and slides**: Disclose when AI substantially shaped the content
- **Assessment design**: Disclose AI assistance in rubric and exam development
- **Student feedback**: Disclose if AI drafted substantive feedback returned to students

Suggested disclosure language: *"This [material/feedback] was developed with AI assistance
and reviewed by the instructor."*

## Accessibility — Faculty & Research Contexts (WCAG 2.1 AA)

Faculty and researchers create a significant volume of content consumed by students,
conference attendees, and the public. Accessibility is both a legal requirement and
a matter of academic inclusion.

### Course Materials
- **Documents**: Use proper heading structure in Word, Google Docs, and PDFs. Avoid
  formatting with whitespace or manual indentation — use styles.
- **Presentations**: Slides need sufficient color contrast, readable font sizes (18pt+),
  and alt text for all meaningful images and charts.
- **PDFs**: Exported PDFs should be tagged for accessibility. Scanned documents need
  OCR and remediation before distributing to students.
- **Video**: Lecture recordings and instructional video must have accurate captions.
  Auto-generated captions should be reviewed and corrected before publishing.

### Research Publications and Posters
- Conference posters: Ensure color contrast meets AA; don't rely on color alone to
  encode data in charts and figures.
- Data visualizations: Provide text descriptions or tables alongside charts.
  Use colorblind-safe palettes (Claude will suggest these when generating chart guidance).
- Supplementary materials: Apply the same standards as primary publications.

### When Claude Helps With Academic Content
Claude will flag accessibility issues in content it reviews and suggest compliant alternatives.
For high-stakes materials (required course content, grant deliverables), recommend formal
review by UM's Disability Services for Students or ADA/504 Coordinator.

### Funder Accessibility Requirements
Some federal funders (DOEd, NSF) require accessible deliverables. Claude will note when
grant-funded content outputs likely need to meet Section 508 or WCAG standards.
